01 / Mission

A lunar-analogue rover, built year one to compete.

The Australian Rover Challenge is an international robotics competition where university teams build rovers for lunar-style tasks. ICARUS is our first entry: an interdisciplinary team targeting three tasks this year and a platform built to grow.

A

Compete

Field a competitive rover and a travelling squad of about ten at Roseworthy, South Australia, 18 to 21 March 2027.

B

Integrate

Mechanical, EEE and CS students working as one team, with CNC and mechanical lab access confirmed on campus.

C

Build a legacy

A repeatable rover programme for BPDC, built to grow in the years ahead.

02 / System design

Four groups. One rover.

Mechanical

Chassis, mobility and manipulation for excavation and construction work.

  • Chassis and suspension for uneven terrain
  • Drive train and wheel assemblies
  • Excavation and construction mechanism
  • Prototyping on the university CNC and mechanical lab

Electrical

Power, motor control and safe battery integration.

  • Power distribution and protection
  • Motor drivers and actuator control
  • Lithium battery packs built to UN38.3 documentation
  • Wiring harness and onboard sensing

IoT + Comms

The link between rover and base station.

  • Mandatory base station
  • Radio link for command and telemetry
  • Video and sensor data streaming
  • Operator console for mission control

Autonomy / Core

System-level integration, mapping and autonomous navigation.

  • Mapping and autonomous task software
  • Two CS and autonomy specialists on the squad
  • Systems integration and process ownership
  • Test and verification across all groups
